What the research shows

A university study of how SI joint implants hold the joint steady. It is computer research, not patient results.

HomeRecoveryQuestionsGuides

What was studied

Researchers at the University of Toledo built a detailed computer model of the lower spine and pelvis. They placed two kinds of SI joint implants in the model and applied the same forces to each. Then they measured two things: how much the SI joint still moved, and how much stress landed on the bone around each implant.

From the back

SiJoin

Three spacers placed through the back of the joint, called the posterior approach. In the model they had the stiffness of bone. Two lengths were tested.

From the side

iFuse

Three titanium implants placed from the side, across the joint, called the lateral approach.

Less motion at the joint

Less motion at the SI joint is one sign that an implant is holding the joint steady. Both systems cut motion well below a normal joint. Each bar shows how much less the joint moved.

Less stress on the bone

Stress on the bone around an implant shows how hard that bone works to carry the load. In the model, the bone around both sizes of SiJoin spacer carried less stress than the bone around iFuse, in all 12 comparisons.

What it can’t tell you

  • It was a computer model, not a study of patients. Results in real people can differ.
  • It measured mechanics, which are motion and stress. It did not measure pain relief, recovery or complications.
  • It dates from 2014 and tested the original SiJoin spacer design.
  • Both systems are used to treat SI joint pain. The right choice depends on your anatomy and your doctor’s judgment.
