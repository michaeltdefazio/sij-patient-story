What was studied

Researchers at the University of Toledo built a detailed computer model of the lower spine and pelvis. They placed two kinds of SI joint implants in the model: SiJoin spacers from the back, tested at two lengths, and iFuse implants from the side. The model used three of each, so the comparison was like for like, and the same forces were applied to both. Then they measured two things: how much the SI joint still moved, and how much stress landed on the bone around each implant.

Less motion at the joint

Less motion at the SI joint is one sign that an implant is holding the joint steady. Both systems cut motion well below a normal joint. Each bar shows how much less the joint moved.

What it can’t tell you

  • It was a computer model, not a study of patients. Results in real people can differ.
  • It measured mechanics, which are motion and stress. It did not measure pain relief, recovery or complications.
  • The model used three of each implant. A posterior procedure may use one or two, so the numbers do not map exactly onto one patient.
  • It dates from 2014 and tested the original SiJoin allograft spacer.
  • Both systems are used to treat SI joint pain. The right choice depends on your anatomy and your doctor’s judgment.
